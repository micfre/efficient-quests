# Independent supply requirements

Different quest IDs and item objectives do not, by themselves, prove that separate quantities of an item are consumed. The supported QuestieDB public objective structure records item identity and optional text/icon; it does not supply a structured quantity or a consumed-at-hand-in flag. See [QuestieDB's normalization schema](https://github.com/Questie/QuestieDB/blob/master/src/meta/normalize.lua) and its [public API](https://github.com/Questie/QuestieDB/blob/master/docs/api.md). A quest-log disappearance or inventory decrease also cannot reliably attribute consumption to a particular quest.

The addon recognizes a deliberately narrow, explicit guide assertion that a stated quantity is consumed by its referenced quest. It combines such assertions only for separately accepted quests, provider-verified identical items and the same local source location, without unparsed conditions. The combined advice remains conditional on the guide's claim and explains each quest's stated quantity. Repeated equivalent requirements for one quest use the largest stated minimum, and an observed turn-in causes the remaining total to be recalculated. An item tag or a generic objective is not promoted into consumption evidence.

The supplied TUG TOC lists 80 guide files. A targeted search found no two distinct uses in those files that satisfy this explicit assertion format. Its shopping list gives 15 and 300 Silk Cloth without quest links, while a separate chapter mentions 60 silk for quest 7793; this does not establish that those amounts are distinct consumptions. A Blacksmithing example refers to several Thorium requirements, but its file is not listed in TUG's TOC and must not be loaded implicitly. The supported guide may therefore produce no aggregated requirements until a reliable, applicable evidence source is available. The test examples that exercise aggregation are synthetic.

Flight-path observations follow the same positive-evidence rule. A learned-path reminder is suppressed only for a uniquely matching observed taxi node in the current map and an explicit acquisition instruction. Missing or unreachable nodes are not evidence of an unlearned path. A guide location name or an incidental place name does not by itself identify a taxi node.
